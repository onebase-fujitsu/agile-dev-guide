Facilitation #

Overview #

Facilitation means helping conferences and meetings (MTGs) run smoothly. The person who takes on the role of facilitation is called the “facilitator.”

The Facilitator’s Role #

  • Create a place where everyone can share opinions with peace of mind
    • If some people stay silent, there may be an atmosphere that makes it hard to speak.
  • Define pointA and pointB
    • Clearly put the following two points into words and define them.
      • pointA: The current situation before the MTG
      • pointB: The ideal situation after the MTG
    The current situation is ***.
By the end, we want to ***.
  • Guide participants to pointB within the time
    • Based on experience, estimate how much time should be allotted, and make an effort to finish within that time.
      If you are unsure because of limited experience, consult with the MTG members and decide together.
      • It is desirable to reach the goal efficiently, but the path does not have to be a straight line.
      • The facilitator is there to correct the course, not to make decisions or reach conclusions.

Why Facilitation? #

In team activities, team members hold discussions and make decisions, so the role of a facilitator who keeps MTGs running smoothly is important. Even when it is not an MTG, decide on a facilitator whenever several people are talking.

Key Points of Facilitation #

How to Keep Things Running Smoothly #

  • Invite only the people needed for the MTG
    • For smooth communication and decision-making, judge whether each person is really needed.
      • If there are too many people, communication costs increase and the MTG cannot proceed smoothly.
        Have only the necessary members attend. (Ideally, a maximum of 8 people.)
      • Check in advance what and how much the participants know, and confirm whether they are needed for the MTG.
  • Gather the necessary information
    • Decide on an agenda.
    • Gather information in advance.
  • Organize information
    • Use the following to organize information.
      • Miro board (online)
      • Whiteboard (in person)
  • Set priorities
    • Have all MTG participants think together about what needs to be done to reach pointB.
      • This helps prevent time from being spent on unnecessary topics.
      • Participants can share a sense of purpose and work together to achieve it (achieving pointB).
    • Partway through the MTG, talk about how much progress has been made toward pointB.
      • This prevents the discussion from drifting away from its purpose and diverging.
      • It helps achieve pointB within the scheduled MTG time.
  • Guide participants to focus on the agenda
    • Make sure no one is doing other work (announce this at the start of the MTG).
      • Since unnecessary members should not have been invited, encourage everyone to focus on the MTG so that pointB is achieved within the time.

How to Encourage Conversation #

  • Start with “Yes, and” instead of “No, but”

    • Start by accepting an opinion rather than rejecting it right away.

      Bad examples

      • No, …
      • Rather than that, …

      Good examples

      • I see, that could work…
      • Nice…

    • Feel grateful to people for sharing their opinions.

    • Encouraging participants to first take in the other person’s idea and then share their own creates an atmosphere where people can discuss with peace of mind.

Dealing with Problems #

  • Too many people attend the meeting

    • Check whether every member is needed for this MTG.
      • If all members must attend, everyone needs to be given a chance to speak.
      • Adding even one person increases communication costs.
        So removing one person is more effective for running the meeting smoothly.

  • There is a loud, inflexible stakeholder

    • Check whether the loud, inflexible person is needed for this MTG.
      • Think about what action can be taken, such as whether they will be satisfied by attending or by being briefed afterward, and act accordingly.
      • If they prevent the MTG from running smoothly or are not needed to reach the MTG’s pointB, do not include them as a participant.

  • Participants are very quiet

    • Check whether the people who do not speak are needed for this MTG.
    • Check whether they feel a sense of ownership of the issue, and encourage them to take action.

  • The discussion stalls and does not progress　

    • Check whether the person stalling the discussion is needed for this MTG.
    • Check whether everyone understands the goal image (pointB).

All team members need to work together toward pointB.
