Feedback #

Overview #

Give candid evaluations of and points for improvement on another person’s behavior.

  • Do it on a flat basis, regardless of seniority or position.
  • Always give feedback one-on-one.

What to Give Feedback On #

  • Tell the other person things that will benefit them.
  • Adapt the content to the situation.
    • Strengths / room for growth
    • What is going well / what would be good to change
    • What to start / what to stop / what to continue

The Mindset Behind Feedback #

How can I personally become a better team member?
How can we work better together?

Purpose #

We encourage growth by building on each person’s strengths and improving their weaknesses. This also leads to building a better team.

Frequency and Timing #

  • Ideally, you should be able to give feedback as soon as you notice something.
  • To get used to feedback, hold it as a feedback activity with the whole team once every two weeks.

How to Run It #

Giving Feedback #

Speak up about what you notice #

Examples of what to say)

  • What went well
    Strengths, things that are going well
  • What could be improved
    Room for growth, things that would be good to change

Points to keep in mind when giving feedback

1. Challenge directly
2. Care personally

Bad examples)

  • Giving feedback in front of others… Obnoxious aggression (1 only)
  • Not pointing out areas for improvement because “they’re a nice person”… Ruinous empathy (2 only)
  • Giving up from the start because “there’s no point talking to them”… Manipulative insincerity (neither 1 nor 2)

Frame areas for improvement as opportunities for growth #

Points to keep in mind when sharing areas for improvement: “TASK” (remembered in Japanese as “takomeshi”)

T: Timely
A: Actionable
S: Specific
K: Kindly

Receiving Feedback #

Take the feedback you receive seriously #

Points to keep in mind when receiving feedback

1. 　Empathize with the other person (giving feedback is just as hard as receiving it)
2. 　Listen all the way to the end first
3. 　Check that you understand the feedback (do you really understand what was pointed out?)
4. 　Take it home and think about it later
5. 　Think about how to improve

Points to keep in mind when asking for feedback

1.  Wait 6 seconds (give the other person time to think about what to say)
2.  Be specific about the feedback you want
3.  Choose the right timing

Feedback Activity #

To get used to feedback, set aside time for it as a team activity.
In that case, form pairs within the team and give each other feedback.

Example) For pair A-B

  1. 5 min Time to think about what feedback to give
  2. 10 min A→B
  3. 5 min Q&A
  4. 10 min B→A
  5. 5 min Q&A
