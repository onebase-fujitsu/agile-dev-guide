Structure and Roles #

Core Team #

Product Managers #

Product Managers (PdMs) are responsible for building the right thing: they prioritize features based on business value, user value, and technical difficulty, and deliver a valuable product. They write user stories to share information accurately within the team, and after development is complete, they perform acceptance checks to confirm that the product delivers value as intended. In addition to communication within the Core team, PdMs hold CL meetings and Inception/Outception sessions to coordinate information between the Core team and Stakeholders/Informed. Because they have a grasp of a wide range of information, PdMs often take the lead in facilitating meetings overall (things go more smoothly when the person who understands the big picture facilitates). Also, when no Designer is involved, they need to provide the information Devs need to implement design stories, for example by creating mockups.

Because they must always make the best decisions based on a variety of user, business, and technical information while assessing the team’s direction and current situation, this is a position that requires not only domain knowledge but also experience and wisdom. It requires the understanding and flexibility to see things from various perspectives, preparation such as agendas, and the communication skills and adaptability to make decisions and respond on the spot while facilitating.

A customer who has business knowledge and can make business decisions joins the team, and each team is staffed by one pair: one person from Fujitsu and one from the customer.

Product Manager

Developers #

Developers (Devs) are responsible for building the product right. In addition to developing the application while evaluating and making use of SaaS and PaaS to realize user stories, they take the lead in deciding the testing policy to ensure quality and build CI/CD pipelines.

A broad technology stack is required: adopting DDD (Domain-Driven Design) to design the overall system, understanding the current system, using the cloud, using different programming languages for backend and frontend development, and so on. It is not mandatory for every Dev to cover all of these, but the aim is for the team as a whole to be able to handle them without knowledge being concentrated in specific members, so it is necessary to learn and acquire unfamiliar technologies and knowledge. Also, understanding the value the product delivers through user stories and communication with PdMs and Designers, and having a feel for technical risks from past experience, lead to fine-grained decisions during development and to noticing issues and constraints in edge cases, which makes them important for high performance.

Each team is staffed by 2–3 pairs (4–6 people) from Fujitsu.

Developer

(Optional) Designers #

Designers are responsible for building the right thing by understanding users and delivering value to them through the product. When users’ work and screen designs are revisited along with the modernization, Designers validate hypotheses through user research, understand users’ problems and consider solutions, and create design principles and mockups.

A customer who understands the users’ work joins the team, and each team is staffed by one pair: one person from Fujitsu and one from the customer.

Designer

Stakeholders #

Stakeholders are responsible for leading the project to success. They drive and manage the project as a whole by sharing information with the Core team at CL meetings and, when needed, joining the morning daily standup to gather information and check on issues.

So that the Core team can focus on developing the product, Stakeholders are expected to provide support for resolving issues like the following that the Core team cannot solve on its own, reduce interruptions from outside the Core team, and behave in ways that ensure psychological safety.

  • Example 1: Money-related issues such as licenses
  • Example 2: Coordination with SMEs whom core members find hard to contact directly

Infra Team #

The Infra team is responsible for building the system infrastructure and operations. They build the infrastructure the Core team needs when adding features. They need to share information with the Core team about the product’s direction, the specific features to be developed and the infrastructure those require, and constraints on building the infrastructure.

Informed #

Informed are people who want to obtain some kind of information through the project. As a rule, Informed obtain information through PdMs and Stakeholders. Ideally, the Core team (especially Devs) should not need to be aware of requests from Informed.

Subject Matter Experts #

Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) are people who have the information the project needs. The Core team interviews SMEs about what the business wants to build and works together with them to organize the business processes. Because SMEs’ cooperation is essential to the project’s success, it is necessary to negotiate in advance to secure their cooperation.
