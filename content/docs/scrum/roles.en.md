# Roles

Scrum defines three roles in an agile development team:

* Product owner
* Product development team
* Scrum master

Let's look at each of them.

## Product owner

**The product owner is the person responsible for the product.** They are also the manager of the product backlog, which is described later.
The product owner is responsible for maximizing the value the product creates by making use of the product development team.
For this reason, in addition to managing the product backlog, they take on roles such as the following:

* Clarifying the product vision and sharing it with those around them
* Setting a rough resource plan
* Managing the budget
* Reviewing the content of product backlog items with customers and end users, and discussing and deciding the order in which they are built and when they are delivered
* Keeping the content of existing product backlog items up to date
* Explaining the content of product backlog items so that stakeholders can understand them
* Checking whether product backlog items are done

Others must never arbitrarily overturn what the product owner has decided.
By making the decisions themselves, product owners can take responsibility for the results.

## Product development team

The product development team can be further divided into two roles:
the **product designer**, who handles hypothesis testing and is responsible for the user experience,
and the **product developer**, who handles implementing the product and is responsible for its delivery and quality.

Let's look at each of them.

### Product designer

The product designer is the person responsible for the user experience.
The product designer comes up with and examines ideas such as "Aren't end users currently struggling with this?"
and "Wouldn't a feature like this solve the problems end users have?"
This is called a **hypothesis**.
Once they have formed a hypothesis, they build a prototype. A prototype can be something like a paper slideshow or a mock-up.
At this point, product developers do not actually build anything. What matters is that it can be given shape right away.

Once the prototype is ready, they interview end users to confirm whether the hypothesis is correct.
This is called **hypothesis testing**.
Things that hypothesis testing shows to be valuable to end users are turned into the product backlog in collaboration with the product owner.

**Product designers model the user experience itself by repeatedly forming and testing hypotheses.**
From the title "designer," you might imagine it is a role responsible for how the product looks.
Of course, appearance is also subject to hypothesis testing, but the product designer's role does not stop there.
The word "design" means planning how something is constructed. It is by no means something superficial like appearance.

### Product developer

Product developers handle implementing the product and are responsible for its quality.
The product backlog lists the specifications that product developers should implement,
but **how to implement them is left entirely to the product developers.**

What architecture to adopt, what framework to adopt,
what language to adopt, what products and libraries to adopt...
Every kind of discretion related to implementing the product is entrusted to the product developers,
and the developer's role is to make these decisions as they go, take responsibility for building the product, and deliver it to end users.

Developers have a very high level of discretion, but at the same time it can be said to be a role with very great responsibility.
First of all, developers must be able to do all the work needed to build the product.
Requirements analysis, design, implementation, server setup, testing, deployment, monitoring... the team is formed to cover every area.
This is called a **cross-functional team**.
In waterfall development, work is done by forming specialized sub-teams such as a "requirements analysis team," "application team," "DB team," "infrastructure team," and "test team,"
but Scrum does not form such specialized teams.
Each development member can do different things and abilities vary, but as work proceeds, the team works to make each individual able to do multiple things as much as possible.

## Scrum master

The scrum master is a somewhat special role that is not directly involved in implementing the product.
**The scrum master's role is to act from a third-party perspective so that Scrum runs smoothly.**
**They are not a manager or administrator, and they do not assign tasks or manage progress.**

The scrum master helps the product owner and development team understand Scrum's rules, artifacts, and ways of working,
encourages effective practice, and protects the product owner and development team from interference and interruptions by people outside Scrum.
Also, while the development team is not yet used to developing with Scrum, they teach the team how to do things,
facilitate events, and provide coaching.
Once the development team can work autonomously and effectively, they give hints for working well,
and help with work when the product owner or development team asks for it.

Whether a scrum master should be assigned is open to debate,
but the author believes a scrum master should be assigned.
This is because, as you work as a development team, the work gradually becomes routine.
And people stop questioning routine work at all.
Routine work no longer benefits from autonomous improvement, leaving waste in the product development process.

To prevent this kind of situation, **a position from which the team's product development can be viewed from a third-party perspective is extremely valuable**.
No one but the scrum master can fill this role.

Because the scrum master is not a role directly involved in producing the product, it is often misunderstood as an unproductive role,
but **the scrum master can be said to be an indispensable role that encourages the development team's self-organization and is essential to improving and maintaining productivity**.
