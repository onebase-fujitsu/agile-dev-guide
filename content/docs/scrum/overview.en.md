# What is Scrum

Scrum is one of the agile development methods. It was formulated by Jeff Sutherland and Ken Schwaber in the 1990s.
Scrum has the following characteristics:

* It maximizes outcomes by ordering requirements based on value, risk, and necessity, and building the product in that order
* In Scrum, work proceeds in fixed, short periods of time. These fixed periods are called **timeboxes**
* The current situation and problems are always made visible. This is called **transparency**
* The team regularly checks its progress, whether the product being built is delivering the expected outcomes, and whether there are problems with how the work is being done. This is called **inspection**
* If there are problems with the way of working, or if there is a better way to do things, the way of working itself is changed. This is called **adaptation**

Scrum only defines **the events the development team should hold, the roles, and the artifacts it should create**,
and the team itself has to think about how to actually apply those rules.
Scrum also does not specifically prescribe things needed to build a product, such as how to write code; the team has to figure those out for itself.
For this reason, it can be said that **Scrum is a framework for agile development**.

The rules of Scrum are defined in the [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/).
A [Japanese version](https://scrumguides.org/docs/scrumguide/v2020/2020-Scrum-Guide-Japanese-2.0.pdf) is also available.

The Scrum Guide is revised every one to two years, and the Japanese version linked above is the 2020 edition.
In other words, the rules of Scrum themselves go through repeated inspection and adaptation.
