# 2×2

## Overview
A 2×2 (two by two) is a technique for making decisions by rigorously prioritizing.
By setting two decision-making perspectives on the vertical and horizontal axes and ordering items along each perspective, you can determine priorities: items in the upper-right of the four quadrants are "what to do now," and items in the lower-left are "what you don't need to do."

## Why 2×2?
You can set priorities while balancing multiple competing perspectives as axes, which makes it easier to explain logically why a decision was made and easier to gain the understanding of everyone involved.

## Key Points of 2×2
- Avoid skew

  When there are many "things we want to do," items tend to cluster in the upper-right, but keep in mind that this is a relative ranking and plot items so they are spread evenly top to bottom and left to right.

- Be rigorous about ordering

  Rather than deciding that items have the same priority and placing them in the same position, always decide on an order and position them above/below or left/right of each other.
  Also, so that items can be divided into the four quadrants, plot them shifted above/below or left/right rather than directly on the center lines.

- Sort along the vertical and horizontal axes separately

  Trying to order items by the vertical-axis perspective and the horizontal-axis perspective at the same time is difficult, so it is better to first sort all items by the vertical-axis perspective and then sort them by the horizontal-axis perspective.

- Set different perspectives for the vertical and horizontal axes

  When you are struggling to choose the two perspectives, it is tempting to set similar ones, but that is not very meaningful.
  Aim to choose two perspectives that compete with (are unrelated to) each other.  
  Also, if they are hard to decide, just starting the work can help you notice what matters, and becomes an opportunity to revise the perspectives along the way.
