# Summary

We have now read through the Manifesto for Agile Software Development and the 12 Principles of Agile Development.
In fact, not a single concrete development method has appeared so far. Everything has been about mindset.

You might be thinking, "Don't tell me about mindset, tell me about concrete development methods."
However, in agile, this mindset matters even more than development methods.
As you can see from what you have read so far, **agile does not prescribe any concrete development methods in the first place**.
This is a decisive difference from waterfall, so many people may find it confusing.
Agile is a development model premised on embracing all kinds of change.
It is best to think of it as taking this form because rigidly prescribing even the development methods would make it hard to respond to change.

In agile, each team autonomously keeps changing its development methods to fit its own way of working.
Along the way, you will likely run into difficulties with how the team is run or how product development flows.
At such times, it is very important to look back on these principles.

With that in mind, from the next section we will introduce concrete agile development methods.
Methods such as "Scrum" and "Extreme Programming" have been proposed as concrete development methods,
and they are widely adopted.

Next, let's study Scrum and Extreme Programming to learn concrete development methods.
