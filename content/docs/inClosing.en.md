# In Closing

First of all, you have read this guide all the way to the end! Great work!
You have taken the first step toward becoming an agile developer.

You may have thought, "If I understand this guide, doesn't that make me an agile developer?"
Unfortunately, that is not the case; this guide is only the first step toward becoming an agile developer.
Nor do you become an agile developer by taking a particular training course or program.

In this guide, we have explained the ways of working in Scrum and XP.
We have also discussed software design that is resilient to change. Is mastering these enough to be an agile developer?

The answer is NO. What is required of an agile developer is "being able to quickly realize and give shape to the value end users need."
Taken to the extreme, this is nothing other than "We don't know what will be needed, so become able to build anything."
Of course, that is impossible.
The knowledge and skills required for modern product development are extremely diverse, and no single person can cover them all.

The architectures, frameworks, and programming languages required differ from project to project,
and all skills and knowledge become outdated over time. Of course, the content of this document will also become outdated eventually if it is not maintained.

What matters here is **an attitude of continually improving yourself as an engineer and continuing to learn every day**.
I would like you to recall the [12 Principles of Agile Development]({{% ref "/docs/agile/principle#a-commitment-to-leveling-up-technology-and-design-enhances-agility" %}}) here:
among the principles, there was a passage that said, "A commitment to leveling up technology and design enhances agility."

Even if becoming able to build anything is difficult, anyone can aim to be an engineer who can build anything.
It is important to take an interest in all kinds of things every day, and to actively learn and try them out.

In other words, an agile developer is **a person who has mastered the various ways of working required in agile development and, on top of that, can keep striving to hone their technical skills every day**.
You have now taken the first step toward becoming an agile developer,
and from here on, your everyday efforts to improve will be very important.

At the end of this guide, we list recommended books. You can read them at your own pace, or hold study sessions with people around you.
To repeat, this guide is only the first step toward becoming an agile developer.
Let's work together toward becoming agile developers!
